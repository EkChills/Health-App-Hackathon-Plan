■ THE PRODUCT
BST — name locked
Photo of a lab report or prescription in. Structured, verified health data out.
Plain-language read for the patient. One-page clinical brief for the doctor.
THESIS: The model reads. The rules decide.
Demo climax = the handoff. Patient shares, doctor's screen fills.

■ USERS
PATIENT (primary): has documents, can't read them, forgets what he was told
Patient's job: "Tell me what this means and what I should do"
CLINICIAN (secondary): inherits a patient with no usable history
Clinician's job: "Give me the story in 30 seconds, and don't make me trust an AI"
NOT a two-sided marketplace. Two views, ONE shared record.
Design the doctor view for a skeptic

■ CORE LOOP
1. UPLOAD — photo, PDF, or typed by hand
2. EXTRACT — AI reads the document into JSON
3. VERIFY — human confirms fields next to a crop of the source
4. DECIDE — deterministic rules engine raises flags
5. ACT — patient view + doctor view
The VERIFY step is non-negotiable. It is the answer to "what if the AI is wrong?"

■ AI DOES (assistive only)
Vision extraction: document image to structured JSON
Confidence score per field — low confidence forces human review
Plain-language explanation of an already-flagged value
Drafts the doctor's summary FROM STRUCTURED FIELDS ONLY
AI never decides urgency
AI never diagnoses
AI never suggests a dose

■ RULES DECIDE (deterministic, auditable, no model)
Reference range — high / low / critical, age and sex adjusted
Trend — rate of change across readings
Drug–drug interaction — openFDA / RxNorm / DDInter
Duplicate therapy — two drugs, same class
Dose sanity — above max daily dose
Red flag — critical value triggers "seek care now"
Adherence — doses logged vs prescribed
Rules engine lives in its own module with ZERO model calls inside it

■ SHARE / CONSENT — the least obvious piece, doing the most work
PROBLEM: doctor has never met this patient and has no account linked to them
How does the doctor see the record for 20 min without getting the password?
Email a PDF → static, no trends, and it lives in a stranger's inbox forever
Give the doctor a login → needs every clinic pre-registered. Dead on arrival.
Patient logs in on the doctor's PC → hands over the whole account, permanently
SOLUTION: patient generates a short code. Like a shared link with an expiry.
Patient picks SCOPE: everything / labs only / last 6 months
Patient picks EXPIRY: hours, not weeks. 24h default.
Code shown as text + QR. Read it out or hold up the phone.
Doctor types the code. Record opens. Access dies on schedule.
Every doctor view writes to AuditLog — patient sees who looked and when
WHY: works day one for walk-ins, referrals, second opinions, the ER
WHY: it is the consent story made physical, not just "we encrypt everything"
WHY: it is the demo transition — the believable reason to switch screens
WHY: the patient owns the record and lends it out. Hospitals do the opposite.
SECURITY: short expiry + rate-limit attempts + bind to first clinician who claims it
Say the security note unprompted if a technical judge is in the room

■ MARKERS — what we interpret vs what we just show
A "marker" = one measurable thing on a report. PCV, FBS, genotype.
A Nigerian lab printout carries 25–40 markers on one page
Supporting one PROPERLY = names, units, ranges, danger lines, copy, advice
That is real work per marker. We cannot do 40 in three weeks.
DECISION: support 3 completely, extract-and-show the rest uninterpreted
"Captured from your report. We don't interpret this one yet."
SHORTCUT: Nigerian lab reports PRINT the reference range on the page
So extract the range from the document. Don't build a range table.
We never assert a normal range — we show the one the lab printed
MARKER 1: Fasting blood sugar — ubiquitous, cheap, diabetes climbing
MARKER 2: PCV / haemoglobin — anaemia everywhere, PCV is what labs print
MARKER 3: Blood pressure — high prevalence, and NOT on a lab report
BP comes in by manual entry — exercises our second input path on stage
Malaria and genotype are recognisable but one-off. Nothing to plot.

■ NO CLINICIAN ON THE TEAM — design around it
Inventing plausible clinical data is what a medical judge will catch
Take reference ranges from the source document (see MARKERS)
Build personas from REAL redacted reports — we all have old results at home
Real reports give us real values AND real Nigerian layouts to test OCR on
Label provenance in the UI: "Reference range as printed on your report"
A pharmacist, nurse or med student is enough. Easier to find than a doctor.
30 minutes over WhatsApp. Keep looking, but don't block on it.
